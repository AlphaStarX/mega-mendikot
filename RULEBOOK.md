## 1. Game Overview

### 1.1 What Is Mega Mindikot 5v5?

**Mega Mindikot 5v5** is a fast-paced, team-based **trick-taking card game** for **10 players**, inspired by the traditional Indian card game *Mendikot*.

Two teams of five players face off across a table, racing to capture valuable cards. The twist? Instead of a normal deck, the game uses a **giant 192-card mega deck** made from six combined decks — which means **identical cards appear six times**. This creates ties, surprises, and a signature rule that makes the game unique.

### 1.2 The Core Objective

The goal of the game is simple:

> **Be the first team to capture 13 of the 24 Tens in the deck.**

Tens are the only cards that matter for winning. The moment a team's Ten-count hits **13**, the match ends **immediately** and that team wins.

### 1.3 What Makes This Game Special

| Feature | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Team play** | You win or lose together with your four teammates. Coordination is everything. |
| **Dynamic trump** | There's no pre-set trump suit. It's **created during play** by the first player who can't follow suit — so the early game is unpredictable. |
| **The Duplicate Rule ("Last Wins")** | Because every card appears 6 times, ties happen often. When they do, **the last identical card played wins**. Acting later is an advantage. |
| **The Kitty** | A pile of 12 hidden cards sits in the middle. One is revealed each trick for the first 12 tricks, and the trick winner captures it — possibly a precious Ten! |
| **Instant win** | No complex scorekeeping. Hit 13 Tens and you win on the spot, mid-match. |

### 1.4 Player Count

- **10 players total**, split into **2 teams of 5**.
- **Team A** and **Team B** sit in alternating seats around the table, so every player sits between two opponents.

### 1.5 Match Duration

A typical match lasts around **15–20 minutes**. Each match is exactly **18 tricks** long (unless a team wins early by reaching 13 Tens).

### 1.6 Brief Gameplay Summary

1. All 192 cards are shuffled and dealt: **18 cards to each player** (180 total), with **12 cards set aside face-down** as the kitty.
2. The **Lead Player** plays the first card of a trick, which sets the **Lead Suit**.
3. Play moves **clockwise**. Each player must **follow the lead suit** if they can.
4. The **first player who can't follow suit** gets to **declare trump** — their off-suit card becomes the **Trump Suit** for the whole match.
5. Once all 10 players have played, the **trick is resolved**: the highest trump wins (if any were played), otherwise the highest lead-suit card wins.
6. The winner captures the 10 played cards **plus** the kitty card (during the first 12 tricks), and any **Tens** captured count toward their team's score.
7. The winner leads the next trick. Repeat until a team hits **13 Tens** or all 18 tricks are done.

## 2. Components

Mega Mindikot 5v5 uses very few physical components — just one big deck and a place to track scores.

### 2.1 The Mega Deck (192 Cards)

The deck is built from **6 standard playing-card decks**, each **modified** to keep only the higher ranks.

**Cards kept in each deck:** Ace (A), King (K), Queen (Q), Jack (J), Ten (10), Nine (9), Eight (8), Seven (7).
**Cards removed from each deck:** All 2s, 3s, 4s, 5s, and 6s.

That leaves **32 cards per deck**, times **6 decks** = **192 cards total**.

#### Deck Breakdown

| Suit | Symbol | Ranks Included | Per Deck | × 6 Decks |
|---|:---:|---|:---:|:---:|
| Spades | ♠ | A, K, Q, J, 10, 9, 8, 7 | 8 | **48** |
| Hearts | ♥ | A, K, Q, J, 10, 9, 8, 7 | 8 | **48** |
| Diamonds | ♦ | A, K, Q, J, 10, 9, 8, 7 | 8 | **48** |
| Clubs | ♣ | A, K, Q, J, 10, 9, 8, 7 | 8 | **48** |
| **TOTAL** | | | **32** | **192** |

> **Why six copies?** Because every card exists 6 times, you'll often see duplicates played in the same trick. This is intentional — see the **Duplicate Rule** in Section 10.

> **The 24 Tens:** There are **4 suits × 6 decks = 24 Tens** in the deck. These are the only cards that matter for winning. They are also called **Mendis**.

### 2.2 The Kitty (12 Cards)

- A stack of **12 cards** placed **face-down** in the center of the table before the match begins.
- During the **first 12 tricks**, one kitty card is revealed **after each trick** and captured by that trick's winner.
- **A kitty Ten counts toward the winning team's score** and can even trigger an instant win.

### 2.3 The Table / Seating

Players sit around a circular table with **10 numbered seats (0–9)**, alternating between teams:

```
                  [0] Team A
         [9] Team B         [1] Team B
      [8] Team A                [2] Team A
      [7] Team B                [3] Team B
         [6] Team A         [4] Team A
                  [5] Team B
```

- **Even-numbered seats {0, 2, 4, 6, 8} → Team A**
- **Odd-numbered seats {1, 3, 5, 7, 9} → Team B**
- Play always proceeds **clockwise** (seat 0 → 1 → 2 → ... → 9 → 0).

### 2.4 Score Tracker

You'll need a way to keep track of how many Tens each team has captured — paper and pencil, a scoreboard, or any counter will do. The score ranges from **0 to 13** for each team.

## 3. Winning the Game

There is exactly **one way to win** and **no way to draw** — the match always produces a clear winner.

### 3.1 Victory Condition — Capture 13 Tens

> **The first team to capture 13 of the 24 Tens wins the match immediately.**

- This check happens **at the end of every trick**, the instant cards are captured.
- The match ends right then and there — even if it's only trick 5. No need to finish the remaining tricks.
- A captured **kitty Ten** counts toward this total and can trigger the win on the trick it's revealed.

**Example — Instant Win:**
> Team A has 12 Tens. In trick 9, they win the trick, capturing two Tens played by players. Their count jumps from 12 → 14. The match ends **immediately** — Team A wins, even though 9 tricks are still left.

**Example — Kitty Ten Wins It:**
> Team B has 12 Tens and wins trick 10. The kitty card flips over and reveals a **Ten of Clubs**! That kitty Ten brings Team B to 13. Team B wins on the spot.

### 3.2 Defeat Condition

There's no separate "defeat" rule. If your team does **not** reach 13 Tens first (or loses the tiebreaker below), your team loses.

### 3.3 The 12-12 Deadlock (Tiebreaker)

What if all 18 tricks are played and the score is tied **12–12**? (This is the **only Ten-count tie possible** — since there are 24 Tens total and 13 is the win target, 12-12 is the only split that doesn't already produce a 13+ winner.)

> **If the final score is 12-12, the team that won the MOST tricks wins the match.** (With 18 tricks total, one team usually captured more than the other.)

**Example — Deadlock:**
> After all 18 tricks, the score is tied 12–12. Team A won 11 tricks and Team B won 7, so Team A is declared the winner.

### 3.4 Draws (rare)

A draw is possible but **very rare**. It happens only when *both* the Ten count **and** the trick count are tied — i.e. 12–12 in Tens *and* 9–9 in tricks:
- A team hits 13 Tens mid-match, **or**
- The score is decided after 18 tricks (with the 12-12 most-tricks tiebreaker if needed), **or**
- Extremely rarely, both Tens and tricks tie exactly and the match ends in a **draw** with no winner.

## 4. Game Setup

Follow these steps in order to begin a match.

### Step 1 — Build the Mega Deck

Combine 6 standard decks. From each deck, **remove all 2s, 3s, 4s, 5s, and 6s**. Keep only 7, 8, 9, 10, J, Q, K, A in all four suits. You should have **192 cards**.

### Step 2 — Seat the Players

- Seat **10 players** around the table in alternating order.
- Assign **even-numbered seats (0, 2, 4, 6, 8)** to **Team A**.
- Assign **odd-numbered seats (1, 3, 5, 7, 9)** to **Team B**.
- Each player should know their teammates and opponents.

### Step 3 — Determine the Lead Player (Lead Selection)

Before dealing the real hands, determine who plays first using a **separate selection process**:

1. Take a **separate shuffled selection deck** (you can re-use removed low cards, or any spare deck — it just needs cards of comparable ranks).
2. Deal **one card face-up** from this selection deck to each of the 10 players.
3. The player with the **highest card** becomes the **Lead Player** for trick 1.
   - Rank order: **A (highest) > K > Q > J > 10 > 9 > 8 > 7 (lowest)**.
4. **Tie-breaker:** If two or more players tie for the highest card (e.g., three players draw an Ace), deal **one more card face-up** to **only the tied players**. Repeat until one player has the outright highest card.
   - *Recursion cap:* This tie-breaker is repeated a maximum of **20 times**. If a tie somehow persists (extremely unlikely), the player in the **lowest-numbered seat** among the tied players becomes the Lead Player.
5. **Discard** the selection deck and all selection cards — they are not part of the match.

> **The Lead Player leads trick 1.** After that, whoever wins a trick leads the next one.

### Step 4 — Shuffle and Deal the Main Deck

1. Thoroughly **shuffle all 192 cards**.
2. Deal **18 cards face-down** to each player (180 cards total).
3. The remaining **12 cards** form the **kitty** — place them **face-down in a stack** at the center of the table.

### Step 5 — Initial Board State

- Every player picks up and looks at their **18-card hand** (kept secret from opponents).
- The kitty stays **face-down and hidden**.
- **No trump suit exists yet** — the match begins in a "No Trump" state.
- Both teams start with a score of **0 Tens**.

### Step 6 — Player Order

- **Trick 1** begins with the **Lead Player** determined in Step 3.
- Play proceeds **clockwise** around the table (ascending seat number, wrapping from 9 back to 0).
- The **winner of each trick leads the next trick**.

### Step 7 — Begin!

The Lead Player plays the first card of trick 1. The match has begun.

### Setup Example

> Ten players sit around the table. During lead selection, the player in seat 4 draws an Ace — the highest card — and becomes the Lead Player. The 192 cards are shuffled; each player receives 18 cards, and 12 are set aside as the kitty. Seat 4 plays the first card. Play moves to seat 5, then 6, 7, 8, 9, 0, 1, 2, 3, completing trick 1.

## 5. Game Concepts

Before reading the detailed rules, learn these core terms. Every term is used throughout the rest of this rulebook.

### 5.1 Card

A single playing card with a **suit** (♠ ♥ ♦ ♣) and a **rank** (7, 8, 9, 10, J, Q, K, or A). Each card exists **6 times** in the deck.

### 5.2 Suit

One of four categories: **Spades (♠), Hearts (♥), Diamonds (♦), Clubs (♣)**. Hearts and Diamonds are **red**; Spades and Clubs are **black**.

### 5.3 Rank

The strength of a card. From lowest to highest:
**7 < 8 < 9 < 10 < J < Q < K < A**. A higher rank beats a lower rank **within the same suit context** (see Section 9).

### 5.4 Ten (Mendi)

A card of rank **10** in any suit. Tens are the **only scoring cards**. Capturing Tens is how you win. There are 24 Tens total. Also called a **Mendi**.

### 5.5 Hand

The **18 cards** dealt to each player at the start. You keep these secret and play from them one at a time over the 18 tricks.

### 5.6 Trick

One complete round in which **all 10 players each play one card**, in clockwise order. A match consists of **18 tricks**. Each trick has one **winner**.

### 5.7 Lead Player

The player who plays the **first card** of a trick. The Lead Player of trick 1 is determined by lead selection; thereafter, the **winner of the previous trick** becomes the next Lead Player.

### 5.8 Lead Suit

The **suit of the very first card** played in a trick. This suit becomes special for that trick — all other players must **follow it** if they can (see Follow Suit).

### 5.9 Follow Suit

To play a card of the **same suit as the Lead Suit**. Every player **must** follow suit if they hold at least one card of that suit. (See Section 8.)

### 5.10 Off-Suit

Any card whose suit is **different from the Lead Suit**. You may only play an off-suit card if you **don't hold** any cards of the Lead Suit.

### 5.11 Trump Suit

A special, powerful suit that **beats any Lead Suit card**, regardless of rank. The trump suit is **not decided in advance** — it's **created during play** by the first player who plays an off-suit card. Once set, **trump is permanent for the entire match**.

### 5.12 Discard (Off-Suit Non-Trump Card)

A card played that matches **neither the Lead Suit nor the Trump Suit**. **Discards can never win a trick**, no matter how high their rank. They are essentially thrown away.

> ⚠️ **Terminology note:** In some card games, "discard" means throwing a card away entirely. Here, a discard is a card you *play into the trick* — it just can't win.

### 5.13 Duplicate Rule ("Last Identical Wins")

Because every card exists 6 times, multiple identical cards (same suit, same rank) may appear in one trick. When there's a tie for the winning card, **the one played last wins**. (See Section 9 and Section 10.)

### 5.14 Play Order

The sequence in which cards are played in a trick (0 = first, 9 = last). Play order matters because of the Duplicate Rule — **later plays have the tie-breaking advantage**.

### 5.15 Kitty

The **12 face-down cards** in the center of the table. One is revealed and captured after each of the **first 12 tricks**. A kitty Ten counts toward your team's score.

### 5.16 Capture Pile

The pile where a team collects all the cards from tricks they've won (including kitty cards). All **Tens** in this pile count toward the team's score.

### 5.17 Capture

To win a trick and take all its cards (plus the kitty card, during the first 12 tricks) into your team's **Capture Pile**.

### 5.18 Seat

A player's numbered position at the table (0–9). Your seat determines your team (even = Team A, odd = Team B) and your place in the play order.

### 5.19 Team

One of two groups of five players: **Team A** (even seats) or **Team B** (odd seats). Teammates win and lose together and may communicate with each other (see Section 11).

## 6. Card Anatomy

Every card in Mega Mindikot 5v5 has a few simple, important parts.

### 6.1 The Parts of a Card

```
┌──────────────┐
│  A        ♠  │  ← Rank (top-left) + Suit symbol
│              │
│              │
│      ♠       │  ← Large central suit symbol
│              │
│              │
│  ♠        A  │  ← Rank (bottom-right, upside-down)
└──────────────┘
```

| Part | What It Shows | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Suit Symbol** (♠ ♥ ♦ ♣) | The card's suit | Determines Lead Suit, Trump, and Follow Suit rules |
| **Rank Name** (7, 8, 9, 10, J, Q, K, A) | The card's label | Identifies the card |
| **Rank Value (Strength)** | A number from 7 to 14 | Used to compare card strength in trick resolution |
| **Color** (red or black) | Cosmetic only | Has no game effect, but helps identify suits quickly |

### 6.2 Rank Value Table (Card Strength)

This is the **single authoritative strength scale**. Higher number = stronger card (within the same suit context).

| Rank Name | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | J | Q | K | A |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Strength Value** | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 |
| **Relative** | lowest | | | | | | | highest |

- A higher strength value beats a lower one.
- The **Ace (A) is the strongest** card of any suit.
- The **7 is the weakest** card of any suit.

### 6.3 Special Card: The Ten (Mendi)

The **Ten** has strength value **10** — mid-range in power. But it's the **most important card in the game**:
- Tens are the **only cards that count toward winning**.
- Capturing Tens is the entire point of the match.
- A Ten played in a trick is "live" — whoever wins the trick captures it.
- A Ten revealed from the **kitty** also counts.

> **Don't confuse strength with value!** A King (13) is stronger than a Ten (10) and can beat it in a trick — but the **Ten is what you're fighting to capture**. Sometimes you'll want to *lose* a trick if your teammate is winning it and you can "dump" your Ten onto their victory.

### 6.4 Note on Card Differences

There are **no special abilities, no factions, and no rarities** on individual cards. The only things that matter are:
- **Suit** (♠ ♥ ♦ ♣)
- **Rank / Strength** (7–14)
- **Whether it's a Ten** (scoring or not)

Every card plays by the same rules.

## 7. Turn Structure

A match is a sequence of **18 tricks**. Each trick follows the same flow. Here's the complete turn sequence.

### 7.1 Overview of a Single Trick

```
1. Lead Player plays first card  →  sets the Lead Suit
2. Players 2–10 play in clockwise order
     ├─ Must Follow Suit if able
     └─ Otherwise may play Off-Suit (may declare Trump)
3. All 10 cards are down  →  Trick is RESOLVED:
     ├─ Determine winner (see Section 9)
     ├─ Reveal kitty card (if within first 12 tricks)
     ├─ Winner captures all cards (+ kitty card)
     └─ Add any captured Tens to winner's team score
4. Check for instant win (13 Tens?)
     ├─ YES → Match ends immediately
     └─ NO → Continue
5. Winner becomes Lead Player for next trick
6. Repeat until 13 Tens captured OR all 18 tricks done
```

### 7.2 Phase 1 — The Lead (First Play)

**What happens:** The Lead Player plays one card from their hand, face-up, into the center.

**Special rule:** The suit of this card becomes the **Lead Suit** for the entire trick. All other players must follow it if they can.

**Restrictions:**
- The Lead Player can play **any card** from their hand.
- The Lead Player **cannot declare trump** with their lead card — because their own card defines the Lead Suit, they are always "following suit" by definition. (See Section 11.)

### 7.3 Phase 2 — Following Plays (Seats in Clockwise Order)

**What happens:** Each of the remaining 9 players plays one card in turn, moving clockwise around the table.

**What you may do — two cases:**

**Case A — You hold at least one card of the Lead Suit:**
- You **must play a Lead Suit card**. You cannot choose to play off-suit.
- You may play **any** Lead Suit card from your hand (high or low — your choice).

**Case B — You hold NO cards of the Lead Suit:**
- You may play **any other card** (off-suit).
- If **no trump exists yet**, your off-suit card **becomes the permanent Trump Suit** for the rest of the match.
- If trump already exists, your off-suit card is either a **trump card** (which can win!) or a **discard** (which cannot win).

### 7.4 Phase 3 — Trick Resolution

Once all **10 cards** are on the table, the trick is resolved:

1. **Determine the winner** (see Section 9 for full rules):
   - If any **trump cards** were played → the **highest trump** wins.
   - If no trumps → the **highest Lead Suit card** wins.
   - Ties (identical cards) → the **last one played** wins.
   - **Discards** (off-suit non-trump) **can never win**.

2. **Reveal the kitty card** (only during the first 12 tricks):
   - One face-down kitty card is flipped face-up for all to see.
   - The trick winner **captures** this card along with the 10 played cards.

3. **Count Tens:**
   - Every **Ten** among the captured cards (played + kitty) is added to the winner's **team score**.

4. **Check for instant win:**
   - If the winner's team now has **13 or more Tens**, the match ends **immediately** — they win.

### 7.5 Phase 4 — Next Trick

- The **trick winner becomes the Lead Player** for the next trick.
- A new trick begins; repeat from Phase 1.
- Continue until a team reaches **13 Tens** or all **18 tricks** are complete.

### 7.6 Special Timing Rule — Turn Timer

To keep the game moving, each player has **15 seconds** to play their card when it's their turn:

- At **5 seconds remaining**, a warning appears (the active player's indicator flashes).
- If the timer reaches **0**, the system **auto-plays** a card for that player (see Auto-Play in Section 8).
- If a player **times out twice in a row**, they're flagged **AFK** ("away from keyboard"), and their turns are auto-played **instantly** until they manually play a card again.

> **For physical (non-digital) play:** Use a sand timer or phone stopwatch. Agree on a time limit with your group — 15 seconds is the standard, but casual groups often extend it.

## 8. Player Actions

There are **four actions** a player can take during a match. Here's each one in detail.

### 8.1 Action: Lead a Trick

**Description:** Play the **first card** of a new trick, setting the Lead Suit.

**Requirements:**
- It must be **your turn** as the Lead Player (you won the previous trick, or you're the match's first Lead Player).
- You must have at least one card in your hand.

**Limitations:**
- You may play **any card** from your hand.
- Your card **cannot declare trump** — your card *defines* the Lead Suit, so you're always following it.

**Resolution:**
1. Place your card face-up in the center.
2. The **suit of your card becomes the Lead Suit** for this trick.
3. The next player (clockwise) takes their turn.

**Example:**
> You're the Lead Player. You play the **Ace of Hearts (A♥)**. Hearts is now the Lead Suit. Every other player must play a Heart if they have one.

### 8.2 Action: Follow Suit

**Description:** Play a card matching the **Lead Suit** because you're required to.

**Requirements:**
- It's your turn (you're not the Lead Player).
- You **hold at least one card** of the Lead Suit.

**Limitations:**
- You **must** play a Lead Suit card — you cannot choose to play off-suit.
- You may choose **which** Lead Suit card to play (high or low — a tactical choice).

**Resolution:**
1. Place your chosen Lead Suit card face-up in the center.
2. The next player (clockwise) takes their turn.

**Example:**
> The Lead Suit is Hearts. You hold 3♥, 9♥, and K♥. You **must** play one of these. You choose to play **3♥** (your lowest) because your teammate is already winning the trick and you want to save your King.

### 8.3 Action: Play Off-Suit (Declare Trump or Discard)

**Description:** Play a card that does **not** match the Lead Suit, because you have no Lead Suit cards.

**Requirements:**
- It's your turn.
- You **hold no cards** of the Lead Suit.

**Limitations / Options:**
- You may play **any card** from your hand.
- **If no trump exists yet:** Your off-suit card's suit **becomes the permanent Trump Suit**. This is a big, match-defining decision — choose carefully!
- **If trump already exists:**
  - If you play a **Trump Suit card**, it can **win** the trick (trump beats all Lead Suit cards).
  - If you play any **other** off-suit card, it's a **discard** and **cannot win** — but it might still be a smart play (e.g., dumping a Ten onto a teammate's winning trick, or getting rid of a weak card).

**Resolution:**
1. Place your off-suit card face-up in the center.
2. **If this is the first off-suit play of the match**, announce the new Trump Suit — it's now set permanently.
3. The next player (clockwise) takes their turn.

**Example — Declaring Trump:**
> The Lead Suit is Clubs. You hold **no Clubs**. Trump hasn't been set yet. You play the **7 of Spades (7♠)** — and **Spades is now the Trump Suit for the entire match!** Even though you played a weak card, you've just defined the most powerful suit.

**Example — Trumping to Win:**
> Lead Suit is Clubs. Trump is Spades (already set). You hold no Clubs but you have the **K♠** (a trump). You play it. Your King of Spades **beats every Clubs card on the table**, regardless of their rank — because trump always beats the Lead Suit.

**Example — Discarding (Cannot Win):**
> Lead Suit is Clubs. Trump is Spades. You hold no Clubs and no Spades — only Hearts and Diamonds. You play the **A♦**. Despite being an Ace, this is a **discard** and **cannot win** the trick. You've essentially thrown it away (perhaps to dump a card you don't need).

### 8.4 Action: Auto-Play (Forced by Timer / AFK)

**Description:** When a player's turn timer runs out, a card is played **automatically** for them using a fixed priority order.

**When it happens:**
- The player's **15-second timer** reaches 0, **or**
- The player is flagged **AFK** (timed out twice in a row — their turns are auto-played instantly).

**The Auto-Play priority order:**

| Priority | Situation | Card Played |
|:---:|---|---|
| 1 | You hold cards of the **Lead Suit** | Your **lowest** Lead Suit card |
| 2 | You can't follow suit, and **Trump exists** | Your **lowest off-suit card that is NOT a trump** (to avoid wasting trumps) |
| 3 | You can't follow suit, and **Trump does NOT exist** | Your **lowest card of the suit you hold the most of** (to minimize the chance of accidentally declaring a weak trump) |

**Notes:**
- Auto-play always picks the **safest, most conservative** card.
- Any **manual play** by the player clears the AFK flag and resets the consecutive-timeout counter.
- AFK players see a reminder and can tap/click to resume control.

**Example:**
> It's your turn. Lead Suit is Diamonds. You hold 8♦ and Q♦. The timer expires. Auto-play plays your **8♦** (your lowest Lead Suit card).

## 9. Trick Resolution

This section explains exactly **how the winner of a trick is determined** once all 10 cards are on the table. Think of this as the "combat" of the game — it's how tricks are won and lost.

### 9.1 The Resolution Steps (in order)

The winner is found by applying these rules **in strict order**:

**Step 1 — Check for Trump cards.**
- Were any **Trump Suit cards** played in this trick?
- If **yes** → the winner is the player who played the **highest-strength Trump card**. (Go to Step 4 for ties.)
- If **no** → go to Step 2.

**Step 2 — No trumps? Check the Lead Suit.**
- The winner is the player who played the **highest-strength Lead Suit card**. (Go to Step 4 for ties.)

**Step 3 — Discards are ineligible.**
- Any card that is **neither Trump nor Lead Suit** is a **discard**. **Discards can never win**, no matter how high their strength. They are ignored entirely in Steps 1 and 2.

**Step 4 — Resolve ties (the Duplicate Rule).**
- If two or more cards tie for the winning position (e.g., two Aces of Hearts, or two King of Spades trumps), the **last one played** wins. This is the **"Last Identical Wins"** rule.

### 9.2 Priority Summary Table

| Situation | Who Wins |
|---|---|
| Trump cards were played | Highest **trump** card |
| No trumps, Lead Suit cards present | Highest **Lead Suit** card |
| Tie at the top (identical cards) | The **last** identical card played |
| Only discards + Lead Suit | Highest **Lead Suit** (discards ignored) |

### 9.3 Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Trump Beats a Higher Lead Suit Card:**
> - Lead Suit: Hearts. Trump: Spades.
> - Player A plays **A♥** (Ace of Hearts, strength 14).
> - Player B plays **8♠** (8 of Spades — a trump, strength 8).
> - Everyone else plays Hearts or discards.
> - **Winner: Player B.** Even though 8 is much lower than 14, **trump always beats the Lead Suit**, regardless of rank.

**Example 2 — Duplicate Rule (Last Identical Wins):**
> - Lead Suit: Hearts. No trump.
> - Player in play order 0 plays **A♥**.
> - Player in play order 3 also plays **A♥** (a duplicate — remember, there are 6 copies!).
> - Player in play order 7 plays **K♥**.
> - **Winner: The player at play order 3.** Two Aces tie for highest, and the **last one played** (order 3) wins.

**Example 3 — Discard Cannot Win:**
> - Lead Suit: Hearts. Trump: Spades.
> - Player A leads with **8♥**.
> - Player B plays **A♣** (Ace of Clubs — a discard, since Clubs is neither Hearts nor Spades).
> - **Winner: Player A.** Even though A♣ is an Ace, it's a discard and is **ignored**. The 8♥ (Lead Suit) wins.

**Example 4 — Highest Trump Wins:**
> - Lead Suit: Diamonds. Trump: Spades.
> - Three players play trumps: 9♠, K♠, and 7♠.
> - **Winner: The player who played K♠** (strength 13, the highest trump).

**Example 5 — Trump Tie (Last Wins):**
> - Lead Suit: Clubs. Trump: Hearts.
> - Two players play **A♥** (the highest trump).
> - **Winner: The one who played A♥ last** (higher play order).

### 9.4 After the Winner Is Determined

1. The winner **captures** all 10 played cards into their team's Capture Pile.
2. If it's one of the **first 12 tricks**, the next kitty card is **revealed** and also captured by the winner.
3. All **Tens** in the captured cards are added to the winner's **team score**.
4. Check: does the winning team now have **13+ Tens**? If yes → **instant match win**.
5. Otherwise, the winner **leads the next trick**.

## 10. Card Abilities and Keywords

There are **no special card abilities** in Mega Mindikot 5v5 — all cards follow the same rules. Instead, the game has a set of **keywords** (rules and concepts) you must understand. This is the complete glossary.

### 10.1 Follow Suit

- **Definition:** Play a card of the same suit as the Lead Suit.
- **Timing:** Required of every non-lead player, each trick.
- **Rule:** If you hold **at least one** Lead Suit card, you **must** play one. You cannot choose to play off-suit.
- **Example:** Lead Suit is Diamonds. You hold 7♦ and J♦. You must play one of them.
- **Common misunderstanding:** *"I want to save my Diamonds, so I'll play a Spade instead."* — **No.** If you hold the Lead Suit, you must play it. No exceptions.

### 10.2 Trump Suit

- **Definition:** A special suit that **beats any Lead Suit card**, regardless of strength.
- **Timing:** Active for the entire match **once declared**.
- **How it's set:** The **first player who plays an off-suit card** (because they can't follow suit) **permanently establishes** that off-suit's suit as Trump.
- **Permanence:** Once set, Trump **never changes** for the rest of the match.
- **Example:** No trump exists. Lead Suit is Clubs. A player with no Clubs plays **7♠**. **Spades is now Trump forever.**
- **Common misunderstanding:** *"Can I change the trump later?"* — **No.** The first off-suit play locks it in permanently.

### 10.3 Off-Suit

- **Definition:** Any card whose suit differs from the Lead Suit.
- **Timing:** Can only be played if you hold **no** Lead Suit cards.
- **Two outcomes:**
  - If Trump isn't set yet → your card **becomes Trump**.
  - If Trump is set → your card is either a **trump card** (can win) or a **discard** (cannot win).
- **Example:** Lead Suit is Hearts. You have no Hearts. Any Spade, Diamond, or Club you play is off-suit.

### 10.4 Discard (Off-Suit Non-Trump Card)

- **Definition:** A card that is **neither the Lead Suit nor the Trump Suit**.
- **Rule:** Discards **can never win** a trick, no matter how high their strength. They are ignored when determining the winner.
- **Timing:** Played into a trick when you can't follow suit and choose not to (or can't) play a trump.
- **Example:** Lead Suit is Hearts, Trump is Spades. You play **A♦** — it's a discard and cannot win.
- **Common misunderstanding:** *"But it's an Ace!"* — Strength doesn't matter for discards. Only Lead Suit and Trump cards can win.

### 10.5 Duplicate Rule ("Last Identical Wins")

- **Definition:** When two or more **identical cards** (same suit, same rank) tie for the winning position, the **last one played** wins.
- **Timing:** Applied during trick resolution, after finding the highest-strength eligible card.
- **Why it exists:** With 6 copies of every card, ties are common. This rule rewards players who act **later** in the rotation.
- **Example:** Two players both play **K♥**. The one who played it **second** (later in play order) wins the tie.
- **Common misunderstanding:** *"The first Ace played should win."* — **No.** With duplicates, **last wins**. This is a core strategic feature of the game.

### 10.6 Kitty Reveal

- **Definition:** After each of the **first 12 tricks**, one face-down kitty card is flipped over and captured by the trick's winner.
- **Timing:** At trick resolution, before score is finalized.
- **Special rule:** A **kitty Ten** counts toward the winner's team score **immediately** and can trigger an instant 13-Ten win.
- **Example:** You win trick 4. The kitty card flips to reveal **10♣**. Your team gains 1 Ten. If that brings you to 13, you win the match right now.
- **Common misunderstanding:** *"The kitty card goes back in the deck."* — **No.** The winner **keeps** it. It's a reward for winning the trick.

### 10.7 Capture

- **Definition:** Winning a trick and taking all its cards (plus the kitty card, during the first 12 tricks) into your team's Capture Pile.
- **Timing:** At trick resolution.
- **Scoring:** Every **Ten** in the captured cards adds 1 to your team's score.
- **Example:** Your team wins a trick containing two Tens and a kitty Ten. Your score increases by **3**.

### 10.8 Lead Suit

- **Definition:** The suit of the **first card played** in a trick.
- **Timing:** Set the moment the Lead Player plays; lasts for that trick only.
- **Rule:** All other players must follow this suit if able.
- **Example:** The Lead Player plays **Q♠**. Spades is the Lead Suit for this trick.

### 10.9 Lead Player

- **Definition:** The player who plays the **first card** of a trick.
- **How determined:** Trick 1 — by lead selection. Thereafter — the **winner of the previous trick**.
- **Special rule:** The Lead Player **cannot declare trump** (their card defines the Lead Suit).
- **Example:** You win trick 3. You are now the Lead Player for trick 4.

### 10.10 Mendi (Ten)

- **Definition:** The traditional name for a **Ten** card. Used interchangeably with "Ten."
- **Significance:** The only cards that count toward winning.
- **Example:** "We captured three Mendis that trick!" = "We captured three Tens."

### 10.11 AFK (Away From Keyboard)

- **Definition:** A status applied to a player who **times out twice in a row**.
- **Effect:** Their turns are **auto-played instantly** until they manually play a card.
- **Recovery:** Any manual play clears the AFK flag.
- **Example:** You time out on tricks 2 and 3. You're now AFK. On trick 4, the system plays for you instantly. You click a card on trick 5 — you're back in control.

### 10.12 Auto-Play

- **Definition:** The system playing a card on a player's behalf when their timer expires (or they're AFK).
- **Priority order:** (1) lowest Lead Suit card, (2) lowest non-trump off-suit card, (3) lowest card of most-held suit.
- **Example:** See Section 8.4.

## 11. Special Rules

These rules cover exceptions, edge cases, and advanced interactions. When a situation is unusual or contested, this section has the answer.

### 11.1 The Lead Player Can Never Declare Trump

**Rule:** The Lead Player's card **defines the Lead Suit**, so they are always "following suit." Therefore, **only one of seats 1–9 can declare trump**.

**Why it matters:** Trump is always declared by a *following* player — never the leader. This is a mathematical certainty, not a choice.

### 11.2 Trump Is Permanent

**Rule:** Once the Trump Suit is set by the first off-suit play, it **cannot be changed** for the rest of the match.

**Implication:** Declaring trump is a major strategic decision. If you're the first to play off-suit, you're locking in a suit for everyone — choose a suit where your team is strong.

### 11.3 Lead-Selection Tie-Breaker (with Cap)

**Rule:** If players tie for the highest card during lead selection, redraws occur **only among the tied players**, repeating until one player wins outright.

**Cap:** The redraw repeats a maximum of **20 times**. If a tie persists (statistically negligible), the player in the **lowest-numbered seat** among the tied players becomes the Lead Player.

### 11.4 The 12-12 Deadlock

**Rule:** If all 18 tricks are played and the score is tied 12-12, the **team that won the MOST tricks** wins the match. If the trick count is also tied (9–9), the match ends in a **draw**.

**Why 12-12 is the only Ten-count tie:** With 24 Tens total and 13 as the win target, 12-12 is the **only** full-depletion split that doesn't already produce a 13+ winner. Every other split ends with an instant win earlier.

### 11.5 AFK Auto-Play Priority

**Rule:** When a player times out or is AFK, auto-play follows this **fixed priority**:

1. **Lowest Lead Suit card** (if they hold any).
2. **Lowest non-trump off-suit card** (if they can't follow suit and Trump exists — to avoid wasting trumps).
3. **Lowest card of their most-held suit** (if Trump doesn't exist — to minimize accidental weak-trump declaration).

**Recovery:** Any manual play clears AFK and resets the consecutive-timeout counter.

### 11.6 Kitty Ten Triggers Instant Win

**Rule:** A Ten revealed from the kitty is added to the winner's score **immediately**. If it pushes the team to 13, the match ends **on that trick** — even if it's early in the match.

### 11.7 Team-Only Communication

**Rule:** Players may communicate **only with their own team**. Coordination — discussing who holds what, planning trump declaration, or coordinating high-low splits — is allowed **within your team**, not with opponents.

> ⚠️ **Note on table talk:** Because players hold secret hands, be careful not to reveal your cards to opponents. In physical play, whisper or use private channels.

### 11.8 Discards Can Never Win

**Rule:** A card that is **neither Lead Suit nor Trump** is fully **ineligible** to win the trick, regardless of its strength. Even an Ace of a discard suit loses to a 7 of the Lead Suit.

### 11.9 The Duplicate Rule Always Applies

**Rule:** Whenever two or more **identical cards** tie for the winning position, the **last one played** wins. This applies to both Lead Suit ties and Trump ties.

**Strategic implication:** Acting later in the rotation is an advantage when duplicates are in play.

### 11.10 No Conflict Resolution Issues

Because the game is strictly **turn-based** (one player acts at a time, in fixed clockwise order) and resolution happens **only after all 10 cards are played**, there are **no timing conflicts, no stacks, and no simultaneous triggers** to resolve. Every situation has a single, deterministic outcome.

## 12. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: Do I have to play the Lead Suit if I have it?**
**A:** Yes. If you hold even one card of the Lead Suit, you **must** play a Lead Suit card. You cannot choose to play off-suit.

**Q2: What if I don't have any cards of the Lead Suit?**
**A:** You may play **any other card**. If no trump exists yet, your card's suit **becomes trump**. If trump exists, your card is either a trump (can win) or a discard (cannot win).

**Q3: Can the trump suit be changed after it's set?**
**A:** No. Trump is set **once** by the first off-suit play and remains fixed for the entire match.

**Q4: Can the Lead Player declare trump?**
**A:** No. The Lead Player's card defines the Lead Suit, so they're always following it. Only players in seats 1–9 can declare trump.

**Q5: Two players both played the Ace of Hearts. Who wins?**
**A:** The one who played it **last**. This is the Duplicate Rule — "Last Identical Wins."

**Q6: I played an Ace of Diamonds, but the Lead Suit was Hearts and Trump was Spades. Why didn't I win?**
**A:** Your Ace of Diamonds was a **discard** (neither Lead Suit nor Trump). Discards can **never win**, no matter their strength.

**Q7: Does a Ten from the kitty count toward my score?**
**A:** Yes! A kitty Ten is added to the winner's team score **immediately** and can even trigger an instant win at 13 Tens.

**Q8: What happens if I run out of time on my turn?**
**A:** The system **auto-plays** a card for you — the safest option (lowest Lead Suit card, or lowest non-trump off-suit, or lowest of your most-held suit). Time out **twice in a row** and you're flagged AFK (auto-played instantly until you play manually).

**Q9: What if the score is 12-12 after all 18 tricks?**
**A:** The team that won the **most tricks** wins. If the trick count is also tied (9–9), the match is a **draw**.

**Q10: Can the match end before all 18 tricks?**
**A:** Yes! The moment a team reaches **13 Tens**, the match ends **immediately**, even if it's only trick 5.

**Q11: How many Tens are in the deck?**
**A:** **24** — four suits (♠ ♥ ♦ ♣) times six decks = 24 Tens. You need **13** to win.

**Q12: Can I talk to my teammates?**
**A:** Yes — you may communicate **only with your own team**. Coordinate plays, discuss trump strategy, and plan together. Don't share info with opponents.

**Q13: Who leads the next trick?**
**A:** The **winner of the current trick** leads the next one.

**Q14: What's a "Mendi"?**
**A:** It's the traditional name for a **Ten**. "Mendi" and "Ten" mean the same thing.

**Q15: Why are there six copies of every card?**
**A:** The mega deck (192 cards) is built from 6 combined decks. This creates frequent duplicates and makes the **Duplicate Rule** ("Last Identical Wins") a central part of strategy.

## 13. Quick Start Guide

> **Goal:** Learn the bare minimum to start playing in under 5 minutes. Read the full rulebook for details.

### Setup (4 Steps)

1. **Build the deck:** Combine 6 standard decks; remove all 2s–6s. You now have **192 cards** (ranks 7–A in all 4 suits).
2. **Seat 10 players** in alternating teams: **even seats (0,2,4,6,8) = Team A**, **odd seats (1,3,5,7,9) = Team B**.
3. **Deal 18 cards** to each player. The remaining **12 cards** are the **kitty** (face-down in the center).
4. **Pick a Lead Player** — deal one card from a spare deck to each player; highest card leads trick 1 (redraw ties).

### Turn Flow (3 Steps)

1. **Lead Player plays first** → their card's suit becomes the **Lead Suit**.
2. **Play moves clockwise.** Each player **must follow the Lead Suit** if they can. If not, they may play any card — and the **first off-suit play sets Trump permanently**.
3. **After all 10 play**, the trick is resolved:
   - Highest **Trump** wins (if any trumps played).
   - Else highest **Lead Suit** card wins.
   - Ties → **last identical card played wins**.
   - Winner captures all cards (+ kitty card, first 12 tricks) and **leads next trick**.

### Win Condition

> **First team to capture 13 of the 24 Tens wins — instantly.**
> If it's 12-12 after 18 tricks, the team that won the **most tricks** wins (a 9-9 trick tie is a draw).

### 5 Key Rules to Remember

1. ✅ **Follow suit** if you have it — no exceptions.
2. ✅ **First off-suit play = Trump** (permanent for the whole match).
3. ✅ **Trump beats any Lead Suit card**, no matter the rank.
4. ✅ **Discards can never win** (cards that are neither Lead Suit nor Trump).
5. ✅ **Last identical card wins ties** (the Duplicate Rule).

### Quick Reference: Card Strength

| Weakest → Strongest |
|:---:|
| **7 < 8 < 9 < 10 < J < Q < K < A** |

- **A** = Ace (strongest)
- **10** = Ten = **Mendi** (the only card that scores!)
- There are **24 Tens**; you need **13** to win.
